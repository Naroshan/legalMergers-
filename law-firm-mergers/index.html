Law firm mergers

The right merger partner for your firm.

A merger can give your firm scale, new practice areas, stronger systems and a succession route. We help you find a firm that fits, and structure a combination that works for the partners, the team and your clients.

Start a confidential conversation → All services

Why firms merge

  • Grow into new practice areas or locations
  • Share the cost of compliance, technology and management
  • Create a route to retirement for senior partners
  • Attract and keep good people with a bigger platform
  • Strengthen resilience in a changing market

Finding the right fit

The mergers that work are built on fit: compatible cultures, complementary work, shared ambitions and partners who trust each other. Getting that wrong is the most common reason mergers struggle.

We start by understanding what you want from a merger, then make discreet, targeted introductions to firms that match. Your firm is not named until you are ready.

Structuring the combination

Every merger is different. We help you work through the questions that need answers before you commit:

  • Who owns what, and how profit is shared
  • Who leads the combined firm, and how decisions are made
  • Name, brand and office arrangements
  • Regulatory steps, including notifications to the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA)
  • Indemnity insurance and the treatment of past liabilities
  • Staff contracts, IT and case management systems

Planning integration

The deal is the start, not the finish. We help you plan the first months after the merger: client communication, bringing systems together and keeping both teams engaged, so the combined firm is stronger from day one.

FAQ

Mergers: your questions.

Anything else you'd like to know? Ask us in confidence.

Ask a question →
What size of firms do you help merge?

We work with firms of all sizes, from sole practitioners joining a larger practice to multi-office firms combining.

Is a merger the same as a sale?

Not always. In a merger both sets of owners usually have a stake in the combined firm, while in a sale one owner exits. Many deals sit somewhere in between, and we can help you find the right structure.

How confidential is the process?

Very. Introductions are made without naming your firm, and details are only shared with firms you approve after a confidentiality agreement is signed.

How long does a law firm merger take?

Most take several months from first introduction to completion, depending on due diligence, regulatory steps and how quickly the partners agree terms.
