Selling a law firm

Sell your law firm with confidence.

Why sell with a specialist

Selling a law firm is not like selling any other business. Client files, regulatory obligations, indemnity insurance and the people who work for you all need careful handling, and a mistake can cost you value or delay completion.

We work only with law firms. We understand what buyers look for, what concerns them, and how to present your practice so that its real value is recognised.

How the sale process works

  1. Confidential conversationWe talk through your goals, timescale and what matters most to you, whether that is price, protecting staff or a clean exit date.
  2. Valuation and preparationWe give you a realistic view of value and help you prepare the information buyers will ask for, so there are no surprises later.
  3. Anonymous approach to buyersWe approach suitable buyers without naming your firm. Your identity is shared only with parties you approve, once they have signed a confidentiality agreement.
  4. Offers and heads of termsWe help you compare offers, not just on price but on structure, timing and what happens to your team, and agree heads of terms.
  5. Due diligence to completionWe keep the deal moving through due diligence, regulatory steps and client communication, working alongside your own solicitors and accountants.

What buyers look at

Buyers usually focus on a handful of things. Knowing them early helps you strengthen your position before you go to market:

  • Fee income and profitability over recent years
  • Mix of work types and how much of it recurs
  • Client base: how loyal it is and how concentrated
  • The team: who stays, and who holds key client relationships
  • Work in progress, unbilled time and debtors
  • Systems, compliance record and indemnity claims history

Protecting your clients and staff

A good sale finds a buyer who will look after the people who built your firm. We take culture and approach into account when shortlisting buyers, not just price.

Where the business transfers to a new owner, staff are normally protected by the TUPE regulations. Where a firm closes without a successor practice, owners usually need to arrange run-off indemnity cover, so finding the right buyer can also avoid that cost.

FAQ

Selling your firm: your questions.

Anything else you'd like to know? Ask us in confidence.

Ask a question →
How long does it take to sell a law firm?

It depends on the size and type of practice and on the market, but many sales take several months from first instruction to completion. We will give you a realistic timescale at the outset.

Will my staff and clients find out?

Not from us. Buyers are approached anonymously, and your firm is only named to parties you approve once they have signed a confidentiality agreement.

Can I stay on after the sale?

Often, yes. Many buyers welcome a handover period or a consultancy role, and some deals are structured around one. We will discuss what you want from the start.

Do I need to sell the whole firm?

No. Some owners sell a department or a particular book of work, or bring in a buyer gradually. We can talk through the options.
